The problem

Two federal mandates. One number. Answered by hand.

The hospital

ICU, operating rooms, ventilators. CMS requires a tested, documented plan.

The installation

10 U.S.C. §2925 requires the hours the mission survives, and what gets curtailed.

Today

An engineer reads SCADA logs for three weeks and makes an educated guess.

Same building. Both filings turn on one question: how long can this place run without the grid? Neither is a dashboard — both are a recurring, budgeted, mandatory document.

Grounded in federal sources

We didn't invent the rules. We modeled the ones that already bind.

The name is doctrine

DoD runs Black Start Exercises to find energy vulnerabilities. NSA Hampton Roads — where Portsmouth sits — ran one in FY2023. DoD-wide critical energy reliability is 99.6% against a 99.9%-by-2030 target.

The topology is mandated

UFC 4-510-01 requires hospitals to have two feeders on separate substations, double-ended substations with normally-open tie breakers that auto-close on feeder loss, and four days of fuel at full load. Four days is 96 hours.

The facility is real

34 buildings, 2.21M sq ft, 110.5 acres per the DoD Base Structure Report. Its central energy plant is Building 20, "CEP20." Its emergency generator controllers are under a $4.7M replacement contract right now.

GAO surveyed 364 installations: 4,393 utility disruptions from equipment failure over seven years, 1,838 of them electrical, over $29M in impact. Every source is cited in data/SOURCES.md, along with a plain list of what we model rather than measure — the conductor list and the ratings are engineering estimates, because as-built drawings for a military hospital are not public.

How it works

The graph is the program. The model is the weather.

Islanding nobody wrote

A walker is filtered to passable conductors. A dead cable isn't an edge it declines to cross — it's one it cannot see. Open a breaker and the campus fractures. There is no connected-components routine anywhere. 

# the only islanding logic that exists
visit [->:Conductor:passable == True:->];

No model touches who loses power

Curtailment is a sorted list: highest tier first, tier 1 never. Out of options, it reports a breach rather than shedding an ICU. That's the sentence that makes the rest trustworthy.

Two model calls, no prompts

A signature and no body. The graph knows kilowatts; it doesn't know surge floods low ground first, or that a fuel truck needs a passable road — so an intact generator still runs dry. 

def draft_scenario(view: Dashboard,
                   lessons: list[Lesson])
    -> ScenarioDraft by llm();

def reflect_on(attempt: str, result: str)
    -> LessonDraft by llm();

Round two receives a sentence, not a score. That's the difference between an agent and a search. Every decision is a graph node citing its evidence — so the filing writes itself.
